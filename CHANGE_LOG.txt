\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Diffusion Models for Generation}

Diffusion models have emerged as powerful generative frameworks, learning to 
reverse gradual noising processes through iterative denoising. Sohl-Dickstein 
et al. \cite{Sohl-DicksteinW15} introduced the foundational connection to 
non-equilibrium thermodynamics, while Ho et al. \cite{ho2020denoising} 
demonstrated that denoising diffusion probabilistic models (DDPMs) could 
achieve state-of-the-art generation quality through noise prediction 
objectives. These models have since been extended to various domains, 
including text-to-image generation \cite{ramesh2021zero}, where their ability 
to handle complex, non-convex data manifolds proves advantageous.

While most diffusion work focuses on pixel-space generation, our approach 
operates in latent space with pre-determined embeddings, requiring explicit 
geometric preservation that standard diffusion objectives do not guarantee.

\subsection{Manifold Learning and Latent Representations}

The manifold hypothesis \cite{bengio2013representation} posits that 
high-dimensional data occupies low-dimensional manifolds embedded in 
observation space. Variational Autoencoders (VAEs) \cite{kingma2014auto} 
learn such representations through encoder-decoder architectures, but impose 
Gaussian priors via KL divergence regularization. While this enables 
straightforward sampling, it can destroy natural geometric structure when the 
data manifold is inherently non-Gaussian.

Invertible neural networks \cite{Ardizzone2019INN, Ardizzone2019GIG} provide 
an alternative by learning bijective mappings between data and latent spaces, 
preserving geometric relationships without distributional constraints. Our 
decoupled autoencoder approach similarly avoids Gaussian regularization, 
instead fixing the latent space to pre-trained molecular embeddings.

\subsection{Chemical Data Generation and Domain Applications}

Molecular generation has motivated specialized embedding methods that encode 
chemical structure. ChemNet \cite{preuer2019frechet} provides deep molecular 
embeddings trained on millions of chemical structures, capturing similarity 
relationships in SMILE representations. These embeddings serve as our fixed 
latent space, bringing domain knowledge into the generation process.

For ion mobility spectrometry specifically \cite{dodds2019ion, hill1990ion}, 
data scarcity motivates synthetic generation, but preserving physically 
meaningful structure is critical. Recent work on decoupled autoencoders 
\cite{dunham2024oracle} demonstrates that fixing latent spaces to molecular 
embeddings can improve chemical detection, providing the architectural 
foundation we build upon for diffusion-based generation.

through their original publication.

This work demonstrates that latent diffusion in a pre-given embedding space 
can preserve the natural manifold structure of chemical spectral data while 
matching target distributions. Applied to ion mobility spectrometry data

diffusion in pre-given latent space can preserve useful information while 
matching target distributions.
